# Context is Key

- Understand what a passage means before trying to apply it.
- There is a big difference between *"What does this mean?"* and *"What does this mean to me?"*
- You should not just read **a** verse. To understand **a** verse you must understand the paragraph that it's part of, the chapter that it's part of and the book it's part of.

# Scripture interprets Scripture

- Use clearer passages that address the same subject to help interpret more difficult passages, reading each in its own context.
- We should use clearer texts with direct application to a topic to interpret more difficult texts with indirect application to a particular topic.
- We also follow *tota Scriptura*, all of Scripture, so we can't ignore some verses in favour of others.
- When we come across a passage that challenges our understanding we must allow our understanding to be challenged.
- Systematic theology is good. Everyone has a system, whether they define it or not. However, we must never let our system become such a rigid framework that we dismiss or shoehorn other passages to fit.
### Read Matthew 4:1-11

# Genre Awareness

- You would not read a poem, a newspaper report, a personal letter and a birthday card the same way.
- Scripture is made up of several genres including poetry, narrative, law, history and apocalyptic.
- The Biblical authors use figures of speech like hyperbole, analogy, allegory and generalisation.
- Understanding Scripture according to its genre is critical to understanding Scripture at all.
- Don't use symbolism to dismiss hard passages, but wooden literalism is equally harmful.

### Read Proverbs 26:4-5

# Consider the audience

- Scripture was written **for** you but it was not written **to** you.
- It is good to reach personal application of a passage to your own life, but that application should be directly driven by the meaning of the passage.
- Who receives this promise, and what are their circumstances?
- What does the timescale tell us about the hope being offered?
- What would the passage encourage its original recipients to do?
- How can Christians receive encouragement from it today without turning it into a personal guarantee of career success?

# Prayerful reflection

- If you lack wisdom, ask God (James 1:5).
- We approach Scripture as people who depend on the living God. He is personally active as we read, and we can rely on the Holy Spirit's help to understand, receive and obey his Word.
- Paul prays for believers to grow in their knowledge of God and to have their hearts enlightened (Ephesians 1:15-19). We can pray for that help as we study.
- Prayer belongs throughout the process: before reading, when we struggle to understand, when a passage challenges us and as we respond.
- Our difficulties can involve our desires as well as our understanding. Pride, fear or attachment to a particular sin can make us resistant to what we read. We should ask God to expose that resistance and give us willing hearts.
- Careful study and dependence on God belong together. Prayer should lead us to read attentively, seek help and remain open to correction. Praying about an interpretation does not make it infallible.
- Reflection means slowing down to consider what the passage says about God, what it reveals about us and what faithful response it calls for. Our response should grow from the meaning we have established using the other principles.

# Read Scripture in Community 

- I believe that part of the reason that Scripture can be difficult to understand is that we were not made to do this Christian walk alone.
- Believers who are more mature in their faith can help you to understand.
- God has given us a church to learn within. Other believers can help us recognise assumptions, notice things we have missed and correct misunderstandings.
- Christ gives teachers to equip his people and help the whole body grow towards maturity and stability in the truth (Ephesians 4:11–16).
- This includes learning from Christians throughout church history. Creeds, confessions and commentaries help us encounter the understanding of believers beyond our immediate circle.
- We should examine the biblical reasons for an interpretation. The Bereans eagerly received teaching while checking it against Scripture (Acts 17:10–12).
- When we disagree, we should listen carefully, explain our reasons from the text and remain willing to revise our understanding.
