# Definitions

### Grace
Receiving what you do not deserve.

### Mercy
Not receiving what you do deserve.

# Grace received, grace given

**21 November 2026 | Bible study**

**Main passage:** Ephesians 4:31–5:2  
**Supporting passages:** Romans 5:6–8; Ephesians 2:8–10; Colossians 3:12–14  
**Aim:** To help us see the grace God has shown us in Christ, and consider how receiving that grace changes our posture towards others.

**Central thought:** Having been abundantly graced by God, we learn to extend grace to others. We forgive from a position of having been forgiven.

## Opening: What happens when someone lets us down?

Understanding grace has been one of the most impactful things in my life, especially in marriage. I know things I have done that I cannot justify, yet Christ died for me. Remembering that changes the position from which I approach someone who has wronged me. It should flavour every interaction of my life.

> **Discussion 1**
>
> Think of a small, everyday disappointment... someone lets you down, speaks sharply, or fails to appreciate you. What tends to happen inside you first? What is your initial response, and is it rooted in grace?

Is our first response to defend ourselves, withdraw affection, or make someone feel the weight of what they have done. These are starting points for honest reflection. Do we have a right understanding of the grace we are under?

## 1. What do we mean by grace?

The definitions at the beginning are useful starting points. They overlap: in salvation, God mercifully forgives our sin and graciously gives us life with Christ. Grace describes God's freely given favour and generosity towards people who cannot earn it.

Our need goes deeper than occasionally making mistakes. We have sinned against God and need His forgiveness. Yet His response in Christ is full of love, mercy, and generosity.

Grace is also costly. The forgiveness we receive comes through Christ giving Himself for us. The cross shows both the seriousness of our sin and the depth of God's love.

## 2. The grace we have received

### Read Romans 5:6–8

[Romans 5:6–8 — NASB95](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+5%3A6-8&version=NASB1995)
> *6 For while we were still helpless, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly. 7 For one will hardly die for a righteous man; though perhaps for the good man someone would dare even to die. 8 But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.*

Notice how Paul describes the people for whom Christ died: **helpless, ungodly, sinners**. God's love reached us in that condition. Christ's death was not a reward for making ourselves worthy of Him.

This is where grace becomes personal. God knows the things I am ashamed of, including the things other people have never seen. His love in Christ is not based on an incomplete picture of me.

There are two responses worth examining:

- **Minimising my sin:** I explain it away, compare myself favourably with others, or insist that my circumstances excuse it. Do I place blame on others for the things I do? In situations of conflict that I cause, is my response, "Well, you shouldn't have reacted like that", and in situations where the other person instigated, is my response, "Well, if you hadn't done that I wouldn't have reacted this way"?
- **Believing my sin puts me beyond mercy:** I act as though Christ's death could cover other people's sins but cannot cover mine. Thought processes like, "I'll just get clean then come to Jesus".

The passage gives us grounds to confess honestly and trust God's love. Remembering our sin should bring us back to Christ and gratitude, rather than leave us rehearsing shame.

### Read Ephesians 2:8–10

[Ephesians 2:8–10 — NASB95](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+2%3A8-10&version=NASB1995)
>  *8 For by grace you have been saved through faith; and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God; 9 not as a result of works, so that no one may boast. 10 For we are His workmanship (ποίημα - poiēma), created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand so that we would walk in them.*
 
The order matters, salvation is God's gift, received through faith... then consequently the life of good works follows. We do not make God indebted to us through our behaviour.

That excludes boasting. There is no basis for approaching another person as though I have earned a place with God through my superior goodness.

Verse 10 also gives grace a direction ->  God makes us alive for a changed life. Grace reaches into the way we speak, work, apologise, and love.

## 3. The posture grace creates

### Read Ephesians 4:31–5:2

[Ephesians 4:31–5:2 — NASB95](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+4%3A31-5%3A2&version=NASB1995)
> *31 Let all bitterness and wrath and anger and clamour and slander be put away from you, along with all malice. 32 Be kind to one another, tender-hearted, forgiving each other, just as God in Christ also has forgiven you.
> 
> 5 Therefore be imitators of God, as beloved children; 2 and walk in love, just as Christ also loved you and gave Himself up for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God as a fragrant aroma.
> Paul names destructive ways of responding to others, then gives a positive alternative: kindness, tenderness, forgiveness, and love. He grounds that alternative in what God has already done for us in Christ.*

**God has forgiven us in Christ.** God's forgiveness is the pattern for our forgiveness and a reason to extend it. We are people who live by mercy ourselves.

**Our identity as loved children.** We begin with an identity God has given us. We can imitate our Father because we belong to Him and are loved by Him.

**Christ's self-giving love.** Love takes a costly, practical form. Extending grace may mean giving up the satisfaction of winning an argument, getting the last word, or making someone repeatedly pay for an offence.

### Control vs joy

> Andrew Wilson (not the YouTuber) uses the example of a toddler throwing a tantrum to illustrate how we often sacrifice joy for a sense of control. A toddler knows that a tantrum will usually not get them what they want, will often result in consequences, and is ruining a moment for everyone, and yet they sacrifice joy for a sense of control. How often do we essentially do the same thing? We won't drop our pride for a moment and apologise, or back down from a petty argument, or hold our tongue from saying the snarky thing... all because that moment we want to feel in control, and so we sacrifice joy and do not exhibit grace.

Let's rather consider these mindsets:

- I can listen because I do not have to prove that I am faultless in and of myself, I am clothed in the righteousness of Jesus, and there is no condemnation in Him.
- I can apologise plainly because admitting sin does not undo God's love for me, in fact a posture of humility helps receive God's love.
- I can acknowledge the hurt someone has caused while remembering their need for mercy. Grace, mercy and forgiveness do not minimise the wrongdoing, but they put it at the foot of the cross.
- I can seek their good even when retaliation feels more satisfying. I know that justice is God's, and I operate from the position under grace.

My own need for grace does not mean we are equally at fault in every conflict. It gives me humility as I address what actually happened. My posture under grace allows for some perspective in the situation.

## 4. Grace in the ordinary details

### Read Colossians 3:12–14

[Colossians 3:12–14 — NASB95](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Colossians+3%3A12-14&version=NASB1995)
> *12 So, as those who have been chosen of God, holy and beloved, put on a heart of compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience; 13 bearing with one another, and forgiving each other, whoever has a complaint against anyone; just as the Lord forgave you, so also should you. 14 Beyond all these things put on love, which is the perfect bond of unity.*

Again, being loved by God comes before the call to live differently. These qualities belong in actual relationships, where we have complaints and need patience with one another.

#### Side point about patience
Bruce Lee was asked how many sit-ups he could do. He replied that he didn't know because he only starts counting when it starts hurting. Patience is similar. When something happens and you react to it, you have not *"reached the end of your patience"* you have been impatient.

**The sequence is important:**
God's love, grace and forgiveness -> Our awareness, acceptance and posture -> Our ability to do likewise to others

Grace can look like:

- Taking a breath before responding. The bigger the "**response gap**" the more likely you have submitted your response to Jesus.
- Asking a question before assuming the worst about someone's motives.
- Naming a hurt truthfully without humiliating the person who caused it.
- Giving a straightforward apology without immediately defending myself.
- Refusing to use a forgiven offence as ammunition in the next argument.
- Being patient with someone's weaknesses while encouraging their growth.
- Showing kindness to someone who cannot repay me or return the act.

We can believe grace as a doctrine while keeping a strict account of what people owe us. It is worth asking whether our everyday relationships reflect the generosity we depend on from God.

### Forgiveness when the wrong is real

> *23 "For this reason the kingdom of heaven may be compared to a king who wished to settle accounts with his slaves. 24 When he had begun to settle them, one who owed him ten thousand talents was brought to him. 25 But since he did not have the means to repay, his lord commanded him to be sold, along with his wife and children and all that he had, and repayment to be made. 26 So the slave fell to the ground and prostrated himself before him, saying, 'Have patience with me and I will repay you everything.' 27 And the lord of that slave felt compassion and released him and forgave him the debt. 28 But that slave went out and found one of his fellow slaves who owed him a hundred denarii; and he seized him and began to choke him, saying, 'Pay back what you owe.' 29 So his fellow slave fell to the ground and began to plead with him, saying, 'Have patience with me and I will repay you.' 30 But he was unwilling and went and threw him in prison until he should pay back what was owed. 31 So when his fellow slaves saw what had happened, they were deeply grieved and came and reported to their lord all that had happened. 32 Then summoning him, his lord said to him, 'You wicked slave, I forgave you all that debt because you pleaded with me. 33 Should you not also have had mercy on your fellow slave, in the same way that I had mercy on you?' And his lord, moved with anger, handed him over to the torturers until he should repay all that was owed him. 35 My heavenly Father will also do the same to you, if each of you does not forgive his brother from your heart."*

Forgiveness takes the wrong seriously. It involves relinquishing personal revenge and the determination to make the other person suffer in repayment.

Unforgiveness is like taking poison and hoping the **other** person dies.

Forgiveness can coexist with truthful but kind confrontation and appropriate consequences. Reconciliation involves both people, and rebuilding trust requires honesty and change over time. Extending grace does not require concealing ongoing harm or making yourself available for further harm. Forgiveness does absorb cost though. It's like releasing a debt owed, you will not be paid back. However, we stand in a place where our debts have been forgiven

For deep hurts, forgiveness may involve a long process of bringing the hurt and desire for revenge back to God. We can ask for help and prayer as we learn to respond faithfully.

> **Discussion 5 — Put it into practice**

> A friend/loved one has repeatedly let you down. They apologise, but it keeps happening. What could a response shaped by grace look like, both in your heart and in your next conversation with them?
>
> How could you be both kind and honest? What would need to happen for trust to grow again?

## 5. Bringing it home

Return to the central thought: **we forgive from a position of having been forgiven.**

God has shown us grace in Christ, knowing the reality of our sin. As we receive and remember that grace, we learn humility, kindness, patience, and forgiveness. We can ask him to make these things increasingly real in us.
